# Common-interface hypothesis reconciliation

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: cross-cutting hypothesis does not pass

## Hypothesis under test

Phase 2/3 synthesis suggested that several technically unrelated positives might be stalling at a common observation-to-decision seam rather than because their phenomena are absent.

Phase 3 preregistered a deliberately demanding cross-cutting pass condition: at least two unrelated positives must map to existing solver decision boundaries using the same contract fields **without requiring a new subsystem**, leaving a concrete soundness/participation/economics question.

The A/D1/F3 census does not meet that bar.

## Reconciliation by rival explanation

### A. Real shared architecture/interface gap

**Not supported by this census.**

All three findings can be described with the same metadata vocabulary (producer, authority, lifetime, consumer, state, counterfactual, abstention, population, cost). That is useful research bookkeeping, but it is not evidence that the solver lacks one shared runtime interface.

- D1 reaches an existing generated-child rank/retain decision with current solver state sufficient for a ranking counterfactual.
- A stops earlier: separator geometry exists, but the dynamic boundary contract needed to make it decision-bearing is absent.
- F3 also stops earlier: the cheap descriptor's reliable untied subset has zero decision-bearing discordance; the tied subset where decisions differ is not reliable enough and resists cheap local refinement.

Only one of three therefore arrives at an actual ready consumer. The cross-lane pass rule requires at least two. It fails.

A generic producer/consumer bus, blackboard, signal registry, or new solver subsystem would solve no demonstrated bottleneck here. It would merely give two immature signals somewhere to go.

### B. Healthy research gating that looks structurally similar

**Best-supported explanation for A and F3.**

Both lines have legitimate positive observations followed by intentionally stronger gates:

- A: coverage positive -> compact dynamic boundary-state contract still unproven.
- F3: representation correlation positive -> reliable decision-bearing opportunity still absent.

Their common shape is the operating model doing its job: observation does not automatically become selection. Calling that an architecture defect would erase P202/P203's distinction between evidence role and downstream authority.

### C. Unrelated local prototype/economics gaps

**Best-supported explanation for D1; partly relevant to A/F3 only after their earlier gates clear.**

D1 has a local ready consumer seam but unknown economics. Its current producer is expensive exact/reference work, its evidence is one-parent despite many rows, and retained data lacks the opportunity/disagreement/subtree-work measurements needed to justify a live treatment.

That is a local information-value problem, not evidence for a general shared runtime interface.

### D. Ontology/documentation incompleteness rather than code/runtime gap

**Partly supported, but already improved by Phase 3 and this census.**

The premise map previously made it easy to put positive observations, candidate consumers and production authority too close together conceptually. P201-P206 plus the consumer-contract schema expose the missing distinctions explicitly. The benefit is methodological: it prevents positive observer results from acquiring selector/prune authority through prose drift.

No new premise ID is needed for this distinction. It is a relation/scope application of already admitted P201-P206 and existing producer-consumer operating rules, not semantic novelty.

## Why the apparent similarity was tempting

All three lines can be narrated as "we know something but the solver does not use it." That phrase hides materially different stop conditions:

- **A:** the useful thing is not yet known in a compact decision-state form;
- **D1:** the thing is known for selected states, and a consumer exists, but prevalence/cost/value/generalization are unknown;
- **F3:** the cheap thing is known, but its reliable subset has no measured decision-bearing disagreement.

Collapsing them would conflate:

- observed but lacking an authorized consumer;
- consumer exists but economics fail/are unknown;
- representation required before a consumer fact exists;
- nominal observations without decision-bearing opportunity.

## P201-P206 guardrails attached to this work

- **P201 causal ancestry:** D1's 18 DEAD rows remain one `R03147` parent; F3 counts parent families, not punctures; A counts levels, not interfaces.
- **P202 evidence role is consumable/path-dependent:** A census evidence cannot silently become a selector; F3 diagnostic agreement cannot silently become routing authority; D1 exact evidence can nominate a ranking consumer without licensing prune.
- **P203 propagation preserves scope/authority:** reports and queue wording retain population, epoch, authority and abstention restrictions.
- **P204 rival discrimination:** the explicit A/B/C/D reconciliation is itself required evidence; vocabulary similarity is not accepted as causal architecture evidence.
- **P205 reciprocal coverage:** this phase uses the current map/queue/source lineage but admits no new premise, so no new versioned map artifact is required.
- **P206 opportunity population:** 121 separator-positive levels, 220 D1 queries, or 31 F3 puncture rows are not substitutes for decision-bearing invocation counts.

## Cross-cutting disposition

The common-interface hypothesis **fails its Phase-3 pass condition**. Do not build a shared signal subsystem and do not turn the contract schema into a runtime API.

The useful output is discriminative:

- **A:** remain at compact boundary-contract representation gate;
- **D1:** proceed one step to retained-evidence consumer/economics falsification;
- **F3:** remain at decision-bearing coverage/reliable-representation gate.

This is a conclusion about the current evidence and architecture epoch, not a permanent theorem that unrelated future positives can never share a consumer contract.
