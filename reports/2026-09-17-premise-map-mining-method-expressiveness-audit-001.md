# Premise-map mining-method expressiveness audit

> **Status:** initial non-blind audit complete.
> **Date:** 2026-09-17
> **Scope:** the preregistered M1-M12 machinery plus the Phase-2 synthesis design from open PR #1849.
> **Quarantine:** this artifact must not be shown to the blind shadow miner before that investigation closes.

## Question

What classes of inference can the existing mining machinery express well, weakly, redundantly, or only by importing structure already present in the canonical map?

This is an audit of the instrument, not a search for additional mining answers.

## Strongly expressible inference classes

### 1. Explicit graph-structural dependence

M2, M3 and M11 can expose propositions that are structurally central, heavily depended upon, thinly supported, or unusually shaped across several recorded dimensions.

This machinery is strongest when the important structure is already encoded as:

- nodes;
- typed edges;
- evidence/status fields;
- semantic-parent/tested-form relations;
- explicit unresolved descendants.

The method is therefore good at asking whether the *recorded map structure* contains load-bearing assumptions.

### 2. Explicit tension and qualification failure

M1 and M8 are well designed for propositions whose apparent conflict disappears after conditioning on architecture, population, budget, epoch, policy, participation, or related qualifiers.

This gives the machinery strong scope-discipline. It is particularly capable of detecting over-broad closure and stale authority when the relevant conditioning variables are already legible in the map or source evidence.

### 3. Taxonomic incompleteness around known semantic parents

M4 and M5 can distinguish:

- one tested implementation from an open semantic parent;
- empty sibling regions;
- repeated failed forms below a still-open parent.

This is useful protection against treating a concrete negative as semantic closure.

It is less a free-form invention engine than a systematic way to inspect the neighborhood around already-recognized semantic families.

### 4. Interface and lifecycle loss

M6 and M10 make composition first-class.

They can express failures where:

- an upstream stage produces something;
- a downstream stage could in principle consume it;
- the relation, transfer, retention, authority or handoff is absent or under-specified.

This gives the mining program a strong vocabulary for losses that do not belong inside any single module.

### 5. Deliberate asymmetry search

M7 gives the machinery an explicit way to inspect paired operations where one side received much more research attention than the other.

This is useful because many research programs accumulate around one operational verb and leave its complement unnamed.

The strength depends on the asymmetry pairs having been preregistered. It does not automatically discover asymmetry axes that nobody thought to name.

### 6. Historical and epistemic caution

M8, M9 and parts of M10/M12 are good at distinguishing:

- independent rediscovery from repeated inheritance;
- current authority from historical evidence;
- research-process conclusions from runtime solver conclusions;
- map salience from truth.

The actual Phase-1 execution reinforced this by retaining narrowing/null findings and refusing to convert replication count into a score.

### 7. Ontology challenge, when the challenger is supplied

M12 is explicitly capable of asking whether the current schema can represent questions generated by alien decompositions.

This is one of the least map-internal lenses because it permits a competing decomposition to interrogate the ontology.

Its expressiveness still depends on the supplied alien decompositions. It stress-tests against a bounded challenge set rather than generating an unrestricted alternative ontology.

## Weakly expressible inference classes

### 1. Emergent structures requiring combinations of individually ordinary premises

The first-round stop rule correctly prevents recursive self-mining, but it also means M1-M12 are deliberately weak at discovering higher-order structures that exist only when several individually unremarkable findings are combined.

Phase 2 was designed to recover some of this through latent parents, orphan positives, evidence dependencies and residual formation.

That creates a useful separation, but also means first-round expressiveness should not be mistaken for total mining expressiveness.

### 2. Unknown unknowns outside supplied ontology stressors

M12 can discover ontology escapees only relative to stress tests someone already conceived.

The machinery has no general procedure for inventing a radically different decomposition of solver research from first principles.

That is exactly where an independently reconstructed peer map is useful: it changes the representational basis rather than merely attacking the canonical basis with a finite list of adversarial views.

### 3. Weakly connected or unencoded conceptual regions

M2/M3/M6/M11 preferentially reward things that have:

- explicit edges;
- explicit descendants;
- recorded evidence states;
- recognizable sibling structure;
- known loci/interfaces.

A genuinely important concept that was only weakly encoded may look unimportant to graph-aware lenses precisely because the map failed to represent its relations.

The method can partially compensate through M12 and source archaeology, but structural mining cannot recover edges that were never represented unless another lens first suspects them.

### 4. Novel latent variables with no canonical vocabulary

M1 can find missing conditioning variables when tensions expose them, and Phase 2 can propose latent parents.

But if a hidden variable produces no obvious contradiction, sibling family, asymmetry, interface break or pressure-vector anomaly, the machinery has relatively little leverage for inventing it.

### 5. Generative counterfactual conceptualization

Most lenses ask what is missing, weak, stale, asymmetric or poorly connected relative to the existing map.

They are less naturally phrased as:

> If none of the current conceptual categories existed, what different set of primitives would make the repository easiest to explain?

That is a construction question rather than a mining question and is intentionally delegated to the independent-map track.

### 6. Search over non-local graph motifs

M2 explicitly rejects one authoritative centrality score and M11 keeps a vector rather than collapsing dimensions, which is methodologically sound.

However, the preregistration does not define broad motif search for structures such as:

- repeated multi-hop dependency patterns;
- structurally equivalent subgraphs;
- cycles with semantic meaning;
- disconnected isomorphic regions;
- recurring relation-type sequences;
- higher-order communities independent of canonical loci.

Such structures can be noticed manually, but they are not a strongly specified inference class in M1-M12.

### 7. Negative space with no named sibling or opposite

M4/M7 are effective when a parent, sibling family or asymmetric pair exists.

They are weaker when the missing possibility has no obvious parent and no named opposite.

A shadow miner using a differently conceived interrogation process may be especially informative here.

## Representation dependence of the instrument

The mining machinery is not representation-neutral.

Several lenses depend directly on choices made during construction:

- M2/M3/M11 depend on node/edge topology and proposition granularity;
- M4/M5 depend on how semantic parents and tested forms were partitioned;
- M6 depends on locus/interface representation;
- M7 depends on preregistered asymmetry vocabulary;
- M8 depends on whether epoch/population/work qualifiers were represented;
- M9 depends on discovery-lineage metadata;
- M10 depends on the supplied research-function sequence;
- M12 depends on the supplied alien decompositions.

This is not a defect. Any mining process needs a representation.

It does mean that canonical-map mining cannot by itself estimate construction dependence. A structurally rigorous miner can still be systematically blind to distinctions the cartographer never encoded.

## Internal redundancy and complementarity

The lenses intentionally overlap.

The Phase-1 replication index demonstrates repeated convergence around the same objects from structurally different directions. That overlap is useful as a robustness check, but it reduces the effective number of independent search mechanisms.

A rough functional grouping is:

- **structure/pressure:** M2, M3, M11;
- **semantic-family closure:** M4, M5;
- **composition/interfaces:** M6, M10;
- **qualification/epistemics:** M1, M8, M9;
- **paired negative space:** M7;
- **ontology challenge:** M12.

Within each group, findings may be independently recorded while still sharing the same canonical encoding and source corpus.

Cross-lens replication therefore measures robustness to lens framing more than independence from map construction.

## Phase-2 expressiveness

The execution plan's Phase 2 materially broadens the machinery beyond M1-M12.

It explicitly permits synthesis around:

- orphan positives;
- latent parents;
- evidence-generation dependencies;
- observer-shaped phenomena;
- endogenous residual formation;
- missing decision state;
- missing experimental primitives;
- information-valued actions;
- generation-versus-selection assumptions;
- decision impact.

This adds higher-order causal and research-process synthesis that the first round intentionally forbids.

It also increases path dependence because Phase 2 operates on Phase-1 outputs rather than directly on an untouched map. A compelling Phase-1 framing can therefore influence which higher-order combinations become salient even when the Phase-2 questions are preregistered.

That is one reason shadow mining should invent its own scheme rather than merely substitute new Phase-1 lenses and then reuse canonical Phase 2.

## Expressiveness summary

The original mining program is especially strong at:

- interrogating explicit canonical structure;
- protecting semantic-parent scope;
- surfacing composition and lifecycle boundaries;
- preserving evidence/authority qualifications;
- challenging the ontology with known adversarial decompositions;
- keeping null/narrowing results visible.

It is comparatively weaker at:

- inventing wholly different representational primitives;
- discovering unknown asymmetry axes;
- finding concepts with few explicit graph relations;
- broad non-local graph-motif discovery;
- generating latent variables that cause no obvious tension/interface anomaly;
- conceptualizing the repository from a basis unrelated to canonical ontology.

## Experimental consequence

Do not use these observations to guide the blind shadow miner.

After shadow mining closes, compare its self-generated method against this audit.

That later comparison can distinguish:

- capabilities the audit predicted and the shadow method independently supplied;
- apparent weaknesses that another method overcame;
- weaknesses shared by both methods, suggesting representation rather than interrogation limits;
- expressive strengths that produced no uniquely valuable discovery.

Until then this report is coordination-side evidence only.
