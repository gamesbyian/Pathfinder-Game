## Two different support questions

The repository already has a useful generation-support envelope with:

- supported/unsupported mechanics;
- grid sizes;
- topology families.

That answers:

> can this source construction represent the claim's basic puzzle/mechanic domain?

A transfer claim often needs a second question:

> does the source actually cover the **response region** in which the claimed solver effect matters?

Those are not the same thing.

A generator may support portals and 15x15 boards yet produce no levels with:

- comparable remaining-path lengths;
- comparable intersection demand;
- similar gate count;
- similar mechanic density/composition;
- the target search stage ever reached;
- meaningful nonzero treatment opportunity;
- comparable difficulty/work-response.

Such a source is syntactically supported but scientifically weak transport evidence.

## Response-region dimensions

The useful dimensions depend on the claim. Candidate dimensions include:

- required path length / remaining length;
- required/self-intersection demand;
- gate count;
- portal/filter/flipper/must-pass/must-cross density and composition;
- topology family;
- routing regime;
- production stage reach;
- treatment/action opportunity;
- work/dose distribution;
- residual difficulty / baseline solve response.

No global checklist is mandatory. A transfer design should freeze the subset causally relevant to its claim.

## H3 example

The H3 remaining-length transfer is intentionally:

- fresh/disjoint at row level;
- same broader eligibility frame;
- same phenotype screen;
- same completion-search operator;
- same length representation;
- same allocation simulation.

That is a clean test of **population recurrence within one source/technique frame**, not broad external transport.

The preflight now requires descriptive comparison of:

- remaining-length distribution;
- reconstructable fraction;
- reach-node/work distribution;
- beam-cull/routing regime;
- path/intersection range where available;
- mechanics/gate-count mix where already retained.

These checks cannot post-hoc filter the cohort or alter the decision rule. Their job is to bound interpretation.

## Relationship to independence

Support overlap and independence answer different questions.

A sample can be:

- highly independent but poorly supported for the target response region;
- strongly overlapping/supportive but heavily common-mode;
- both;
- neither.

For example, a completely different generator may provide construction independence while missing the treatment opportunity entirely. Conversely, fresh rows from the same generator may strongly test recurrence at the exact response boundary while offering little source-construction independence.

Therefore “cross-generator” is not automatically stronger evidence than “same-generator fresh population.” Strength is claim-relative.

## Opportunity support

For solver mechanisms, **opportunity support** is especially important.

A treatment cannot fail meaningfully on rows where:

- its stage is never reached;
- its action is never scheduled;
- its node/work allocation is zero;
- a prerequisite branch never occurs;
- upstream budget starvation prevents the decision seam.

The existing resolution-envelope work already treats reach/participation/measurement support as inference gates. Transfer support should reuse that logic conceptually rather than inventing a parallel “generator coverage” vocabulary for treatment opportunity.

## Why no shared transport schema yet

The current examples differ too much:

- generation support cares about representable mechanics/topology;
- H3 cares about remaining-length/work response;
- D1 cares about eligible production cull decisions;
- Class-3 dose cares about exact-action exposure;
- topology transfer cares about decision-bearing discordance and descriptor support.

A universal object would either omit decisive dimensions or become a nullable catalogue.

Promotion trigger for a shared machine owner:

- at least two live transfer designs independently encode the same response-region fields with the same semantics and validators.

Until then, transfer preflights should state support dimensions directly.

## Practical rule

For any proposed transfer, record three separate things:

1. **ancestry independence** — what changed between source and target;
2. **domain support** — whether the target/source can represent the relevant mechanics/topology;
3. **response-region overlap** — whether the actual causal opportunity and response range are present.

Do not let any one of those stand in for the other two.
