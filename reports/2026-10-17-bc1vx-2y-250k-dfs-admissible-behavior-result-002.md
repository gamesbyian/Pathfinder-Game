## A real behavior-changing experiment, not a passive count

Previously [2Y-001](2026-10-17-bc1vx-2y-shared-stage-dfs-admissible-opportunity-result-001.md) established **535** potential BC1-VX volume-reject *events* across an eight-parent **25k** passive DFS/admissible canary. Those events passed every earlier shared hard prune and successful ordinary connectivity but were only observed.

This successor changes exactly one decision under a disposable experiment-only `--bc1-shared-transfer-prune` option: after successful ordinary connectivity at the end of `evaluatePrunedMove`, compute the same typed BC1-VX stranded-fresh-volume value; if the goal-free stranded fresh cells leave insufficient capacity for remaining counted steps/intersections, reject the candidate. All other caller budget, level, score, move legality, attempt order and pruning policy are source-identical across control/treatment. No historical hints select actions and stochastic repair remains excluded. The full [one-shot run](https://github.com/gamesbyian/Pathfinder-Game/actions/runs/39076954725) used 250k canonical work/level/method with a 600s safety deadline; every case finished validly below 1% atomic cap overshoot, zero deadline truncations and no tool errors.

## Results

| Isolated technique | Control solves | Treatment solves | True BC1-VX rejection events | Control canonical work | Treatment canonical work | Aggregate reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DFS `objectiveFirst` | **0/8** | **0/8** | **1,628** | 2,002,161 | 2,001,998 | **0.0081%** |
| Admissible-order `default` | **2/8** | **2/8** | **2,835** | 1,546,721 | 1,546,186 | **0.0346%** |
| Both method observations | **2/16** | **2/16** | **4,463** | 3,548,882 | 3,548,184 | **0.0197%** |

There are **zero unique treatment wins and zero control losses** on the selected eight parent levels; treating the 16 method-cells as independent puzzle samples would be incorrect. Both prior admissible winners remain valid and have **identical winning packed paths** across arms. `R02941`: 3,440 → 3,298 canonical work (142 saved); `R02307`: 42,573 → 42,026 (547 saved). Thus **689 of the aggregate 698 saved units** came from two already-solvable cases. For the 14 work-capped unsolved technique-cells, near-identical consumed budgets are expected by construction; the experiment does not resolve whether more useful states were reached near the work cap.

Sequential, nonrandomized observed wall elapsed sums were DFS **2,335 → 2,573 ms** and admissible **1,321 → 1,429 ms** (+346 ms or +9.5% overall). They show an adverse possible cost direction for an additional unconditional full graph pass but are **not** a controlled CPU benchmark or a valid cross-machine latency estimate. A 0.0197% canonical-work reduction cannot by itself repay a real ~9.5% CPU penalty if it generalizes; both quantities require better pricing before deployment.

## What remains valuable

The BC1-VX theorem is still useful. Its **beam** implementation was previously promoted with accepted extra cold solves and cross-cohort fixed-work gains. This experiment only closes the **specific always-on shared DFS/admissible post-connectivity extra graph scan** at the 250k test dose. It does not falsify the underlying graph theorem, eliminate the possibility of occasional deeper cold rescue, or refute a version with much cheaper conditional invocation.

Do **not** rerun the same unconditional scan at 50M simply to hunt for a rare rescue after the economical warning. A successor should aim for measurable *avoidable* work at a sharply lower CPU price: a quick state-local prefilter from the original connectivity volume slack, a cached/partial cut summary, or triggering only during a narrow high-risk stage. It would need its own correctness proof and independently paired parent sample before any production change. PG-K's similarly negative hint-guided exact restoration remains distinct and should not be conflated with this 2Y economy result.
