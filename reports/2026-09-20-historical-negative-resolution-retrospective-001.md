## Purpose

The shared resolution envelope should prove useful on evidence that already fooled or constrained the research program, not merely on new experiments designed around it.

This pass therefore asks of several influential recent closures:

1. Was the target phenomenon observable for the actual claim?
2. Did the chosen observable discriminate the important rivals?
3. If the result was negative, what exact claim was resolved?
4. Did later authority preserve that scope?
5. Would the shared envelope have prevented a known mistaken inference?

## Calibration case: the old 0/483 work-ladder false negative

The strongest calibration case is embedded in
`reports/2026-09-18-ws2-post-d1-work-ladder-reuse-and-confirmation-preflight-001.md`.

### Historical reuse observation

A July high-budget artifact overlapped 483 current-residual ids:

- 0/483 solved;
- 457 had been attempted at 1.2B nodes;
- 449 cleanly reached the node ceiling;
- only 34 were wall/infra censored.

Looked at superficially, this is a formidable negative: large N, enormous dose, and mostly uncensored outcomes.

It was nevertheless **not resolution-ready for the claim "today's level-blind solver lacks recoverable capacity at 4x budget."**

### Broken fidelity

Two execution-fidelity conditions were wrong:

1. **solver revision drift:** the artifact was produced about seven weeks earlier, before many solver changes;
2. **execution-regime drift:** it used history-aware `--resume --save-hints` execution rather than the current level-blind cold procedure whose budget response was the question.

Those are not ordinary missing rows or low participation. They mean the intervention being observed was not the intervention named by the current claim.

In resolution-envelope terms:

- coverage: largely satisfied;
- participation/dose: satisfied for most rows;
- censoring: mostly satisfied and explicitly accounted for;
- **fidelity: blocked**;
- current-claim resolution: blocked.

The later precommitted current-commit, level-blind 20-id slice found 3/20 solves at 1.2B after 0/20 at 300M, directly overturning the apparent "absent capability" reading.

This is why fidelity/comparability now has its own shared observability axis instead of being overloaded into eligibility.

## Case: Class-2 must-turn economics

`reports/2026-09-16-class2-must-turn-economics-result-001.md`

Verdict: **clean, resolution-ready negative for the stated economics form.**

Why:

- frozen bounded eligible population;
- exact population coverage 60/60 on both arms;
- genuine target-stage participation on 60/60 treatment rows;
- zero errors/truncations;
- referee-valid solved rows;
- explicit gain and collateral strata;
- fixed-work/collateral discriminator directly separates unique gain from downstream solve displacement.

The result is also identified correctly:

- 4 target-stage solves exist;
- all four merely intercept rows solved later by control;
- zero of 43 gain rows are rescued;
- aggregate work rises ~6.5%.

So "the mechanism has no capability" would be wrong, but "this unconditional 7M placement has negative fixed-work economics on this eligible population" is well identified.

The question registry preserves exactly that distinction and keeps R02768/R02180 as capability/placement evidence.

No reopening is warranted from this audit.

## Case: H2 structural-stasis signals

`reports/2026-09-17-h2-structural-stasis-observer-result-001.md`

Verdict: **clean tested-form negative.**

The important discriminator was predictive value, not existence of marginal continuation value.

There is ample outcome headroom: 22/28 levels improve badness at 10x work. The two proposed cheap stasis signals simply fail to predict which levels benefit, on both the development and untouched confirmation halves.

This matters because a zero correlation cannot be dismissed as "nothing varied."

The current question state `closed-tested-form` is appropriately narrower than a premise-level negative and reopens on different structural-response dimensions or populations.

## Case: H1 event-feasibility vocabulary

`reports/2026-09-17-h1-event-feasibility-result-001.md`

Verdict: **scope-limited but identified negative for one universal compact descriptor.**

29/449 queries remain unresolved, so the full event population is not perfectly observable.

But the principal falsifier does not depend on treating those unresolved queries as negative:

- the strongest `E-FLIP-ORDER` pattern reverses direction across independent parents;
- cross/pass event realizability is high but the tested distance-to-goal descriptor does not separate blocked from realizable interfaces.

That is sufficient to reject one fixed universal rank/distance relation without claiming that event realizability or H1's broader premise is absent.

Again, current authority uses `closed-tested-form`, which preserves the correct scope.

## Case: 4x work-ladder economics

`reports/2026-09-19-ws2-work-ladder-4x-only-matched-work-costing-result-001.md`

Verdict: **resolution-ready for the economics question, not for capacity.**

The report explicitly accepts the positive capacity result and asks a different question: whether buying it by 4x node escalation is competitive with cheaper already-observed alternative technique investments in the same canonical work currency.

The observed cost gap is large:

- ~14.1B work per marginal recovered solve at 4x;
- 24x–635x above listed EW1 comparators;
- still >=3.6x above the most expensive listed comparator under the impossible favorable assumption that all 20 ladder rows solve.

Population mismatch remains a transport/generalization caveat, reflected in the explicit reopen condition. It is not an observability failure for the bounded economics conclusion.

## Case: Lane G real-frontier completion dose

`reports/2026-09-19-lane-g-real-frontier-completion-dose-pilot-result-001.md`

Verdict: **observability-blocked for feasibility, but identified negative for modest-dose cheap local repairability.**

Every 34/45 completion attempt that reaches the completion operator is still ceiling-censored at both 2M and 16M nodes. Therefore:

- feasibility/infeasibility is not observed;
- "these partials cannot be repaired" is not licensed.

However, the 8x dose escalation produces exactly zero response while all 34 remain bound. The decision question is narrower: whether a real production partial appears *cheaply/local-repairable enough* to justify implementing complete-path LNS.

For that discriminator, the lack of response across 2M -> 16M is relevant and the report correctly refuses a further blind dose escalation.

Current workstream authority preserves the distinction explicitly: "still budget-censored, not proven infeasible, but rules out modest dose as the fix."

No current authority repair is needed.

## Case: D3 mechanic attribution correction

`reports/2026-09-17-lane-d3-segment-mechanic-attribution-result-001.md`

This is not a negative, but it exposes the next downstream epistemic hazard.

An earlier report described a zero-legal result across 10 flipper-bearing levels / 2,152 candidates. The segment-level follow-up found that only 3 of those levels produced qualifying length-matched candidates at all, with one level supplying 70% of those candidates.

The zero remains true, but its independent support is much thinner than the raw row count suggested.

This is not primarily observability or identifiability. It is **independence / causal ancestry**: how many genuinely independent pieces of evidence support a conclusion, and what shared parent/construction/code ancestry makes apparent replication less independent than row count implies.

That is the correct next concept after the current observability/identifiability work.

## Retrospective result

The new framework does **not** reveal a cemetery of wrongly closed questions.

The more interesting result is:

- strong recent reports are generally careful about scope;
- current authorities usually preserve that scope;
- one known false negative cleanly demonstrates the need for a missing fidelity/comparability axis;
- several reports demonstrate that a top-level negative can be valid for a decision discriminator while the underlying feasibility/capability remains unresolved;
- D3 shows the next major risk is apparent evidence multiplicity without genuine independence.

## Standing retrospective rule

When an old negative becomes decision-relevant again:

1. reconstruct the exact claim, not merely the report status;
2. reconstruct its required resolution envelope;
3. treat solver/config/protocol/intervention mismatch as **fidelity failure**, not generic uncertainty;
4. distinguish a negative discriminator from a negative capability/feasibility claim;
5. inspect effective independent units and shared ancestry before treating row count as replication;
6. reopen only the scope that the old evidence failed to resolve.

Do not blanket-retrofit old reports merely to make history uniform.
