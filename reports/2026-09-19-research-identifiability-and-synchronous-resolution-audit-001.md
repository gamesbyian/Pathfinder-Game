## The next concept

The next major concept after **observability** is **identifiability**, with **discriminability** as its operational test.

Observability asks:

> Could the phenomenon have produced an interpretable observation under this execution and measurement envelope?

Identifiability asks:

> If we obtain this observation, does it distinguish the explanation we care about from the important rivals?

A perfectly observable result can still be scientifically weak when several rival mechanisms predict the same pattern.

Examples already recurring in Pathfinder include:

- absent capability vs underdose;
- search-quality failure vs allocation failure;
- representation failure vs routing/exposure failure;
- mechanic-specific causality vs generic difficulty easing;
- causal false-negative rejection vs harmless witness divergence;
- independent additive effects vs synergy vs interference;
- genuinely distinct policy behavior vs renamed/redundant configuration;
- current capability vs replay/provenance-selected historical success.

## Why this concept is already real

### Inference-audit framework

The framework has an explicit "Rival explanations and discriminability" section and says:

- do not settle for descriptive labels when a causal distinction matters;
- state the observation that separates the important rivals;
- keep conclusions narrow when retained evidence cannot discriminate them;
- buy new compute only when an unresolved rival can change a current or plausible next action.

### Research-question contract

The shared question contract already requires:

- `liveAmbiguity`;
- `discriminatingObservable`;
- `outcomeInterpretation`;
- optional measurement-opportunity identity.

So the machine vocabulary for a discrimination problem already exists.

### Measurement-opportunity overlay

Every MO is admitted only when:

- a live mapped ambiguity exists;
- an observable can discriminate materially different live explanations;
- the observable is not decision-equivalent to retained telemetry;
- the result can change a decision/reopen/experiment.

The overlay then explicitly lists what each observable `distinguishes`.

### Live WS2 work

The failure-response analysis contract already carries:

- live rivals;
- primary discriminator;
- negative-resolution semantics;
- a decision purpose of scientific-question discrimination.

The work-ladder result is a concrete example: the observation discriminated "absent capability" from "underdose/threshold" on the tested slice.

This is therefore not a new ontology. It is another repeated scientific invariant whose machinery is distributed.

## Why observability and identifiability should be explored synchronously

They are mutually constraining.

### Identifiability determines the required observability envelope

There is no context-free list of observability axes that every experiment must satisfy.

The live rivals determine which axes matter.

#### Example: absent capability vs underdose

A useful discriminator is budget response.

Required observability includes:

- solver/config applicability;
- enough work-dose separation;
- non-binding or explicitly modeled censoring;
- real execution participation;
- stable population identity.

Stage-specific reach may be irrelevant if the claim is whole-solver budget elasticity.

#### Example: allocation failure vs representation failure

A useful discriminator may be decision-point exposure plus counterfactual retention/rejection evidence.

Required observability includes:

- reaching the implicated decision point;
- real candidate exposure;
- instrument support at that decision;
- enough headroom for the alternative to matter.

A generic whole-run solve/null is observable in a broad sense but not observable **for this discriminator**.

#### Example: mechanic-specific causality vs generic easing

The discriminator requires a controlled intervention that changes the mechanic relation while controlling generic difficulty.

Required observability therefore includes:

- the mechanic being present/applicable;
- intervention fidelity;
- matched difficulty/context controls;
- supported outcome/reference measurement.

An ordinary solve-rate change is not identifiable even when perfectly observed.

### Observability constrains which discriminators are affordable or real

A theoretically beautiful discriminator is useless if:

- the population almost never reaches the relevant stage;
- the needed mechanic is absent from the available generator;
- the observer cannot support the measurement;
- exact/reference labels abstain on most cases;
- censoring dominates before the discriminator can express itself.

So an observability audit can prune discriminators before expensive measurement.

## Joint concept: epistemic resolution

The two lenses can be explored through one compact loop:

1. **State the live ambiguity.**
2. **Name the rival explanations.**
3. **Specify the smallest observation whose outcomes differ across those rivals.**
4. **Derive the observability preconditions required for that discriminator.**
5. **Check whether current evidence/population/instrument can satisfy those preconditions.**
6. If not, classify the observability blocker and repair/acquire the smallest missing capability.
7. If yes, collect the smallest discriminating evidence.
8. **Map each possible outcome back to rival survival/elimination/narrowing.**
9. Only then decide whether the result changes queue priority, premise status, experiment design, or implementation.

This is an **epistemic resolution loop** rather than two sequential checklists.

## The synchronous matrix

A simple 2×2 view is useful:

| | Discriminator weak/unavailable | Discriminator strong |
|---|---|---|
| **Observability weak** | Question is not experimentally ready. Refine rivals/discriminator while repairing the smallest observability blocker. | Measurement target is conceptually clear but unavailable. Acquisition/instrumentation/exposure work is well-directed. |
| **Observability strong** | Evidence can be collected, but it will not resolve the important ambiguity. Design a better contrast instead of scaling N. | Resolution-ready. Run the smallest decision-changing experiment/analysis. |

There is no scientifically useful quadrant where "collect lots of data anyway" is the default.

## Why synchronous exploration is better than serial exploration

### It prevents overbuilding observability

A generic observability system could demand seven axes for every question.

The discriminator tells us which subset is actually required.

This keeps the observability envelope claim-relative rather than ceremonial.

### It prevents elegant but unmeasurable discriminators

A rival-separating observable can look excellent on paper while requiring a population or instrument Pathfinder does not possess.

Observability feasibility tests that immediately.

### It turns capability acquisition into question-directed work

If the discriminator is known but unobservable, the missing observability axis names the capability to acquire:

- new generator/source support;
- new telemetry;
- exact/reference support;
- stage exposure;
- allocation headroom;
- uncensored work envelope.

This is much more directed than generic infrastructure improvement.

### It improves experiment economics

Before buying N:

- ask whether enough rows can express the discriminator;
- size on discriminator-bearing opportunities rather than raw rows;
- separate effect-bearing cohorts from representative safety/generalization cohorts.

That ties the existing opportunity-sizing work directly to scientific discrimination.

## Proposed paired artifact, not yet a new framework

For a live question, a compact paired record could look conceptually like:

```
questionId
liveAmbiguity
rivals[]
discriminatingObservable
outcomeInterpretation{}

requiredObservabilityAxes{}
observabilityBlockers[]
resolutionStatus
nextResolutionAction
```

Do not add this as a universal registry. The first implementation is deliberately a small shared envelope emitted by specialist producers. It is a derived interpretation surface, not a new scientific authority. Question contracts still own the ambiguity/discriminator contract; specialist producers still own axis derivation and verdicts.

## Candidate synchronous pilots

### Pilot A: WS2 failure-response reconnaissance

This is the best near-term pilot because it already has:

- explicit live rivals;
- a primary discriminator;
- negative-resolution semantics;
- protocol-relative applicability;
- missingness/censoring rules;
- an explicit acquisition/reopen condition.

Ask simultaneously:

- what rival distinction can compact failure response actually make?
- what exact observability preconditions must hold for that distinction?
- if they do not hold, is the blocker acquisition, instrumentation, identity compatibility, or lack of process context?

### Pilot B: admissible-order reserve starvation

This is materially different because the live question is closer to economics/allocation than instrumentation.

Ask simultaneously:

- what observation distinguishes reserve starvation from generic high-cost capability?
- what work/exposure/opportunity envelope is required?
- which result would justify an allocation intervention versus merely preserve historical capability memory?

Those two pilots have now been implemented. Both live consumers emit the same small resolution envelope while retaining materially different specialist semantics, so the helper is earned and exists as `scripts/research-resolution-envelope-lib.mjs`. The result is documented in `reports/2026-09-19-synchronous-observability-identifiability-pilot-001.md`.

## What comes after both concepts

If observability and identifiability are both strong, the next major epistemic questions are:

- **independence / causal ancestry:** how many genuinely independent observations support the inference?
- **transportability / generalization:** to what populations/regimes can the identified effect travel?

Those should not be pulled forward yet.

A beautifully identified effect on one causally dependent family can still fail to generalize, but solving that is downstream of establishing that the phenomenon was observable and the explanation identifiable at all.

## Standing rule

**Design observability around the discriminator, and design the discriminator around an observable decision-changing contrast.**

Neither concept should be explored in isolation when the research question is live.
