# Does R02248's orientation-scoring interaction recur elsewhere? A targeted corpus scan

> **Status:** concluded-positive
> **Last evidence:** 2026-08-08 — reconciled with Phase D in [`2026-08-08-symmetry-orientation-sensitivity-synthesis.md`](2026-08-08-symmetry-orientation-sensitivity-synthesis.md)
> **Decision:** the failure shape recurs, but the responsible score term varies by family; do not generalize R02248's ablation into a global retune
> **Remaining gate:** none for this targeted scan; prevalence measurement belongs to the wide-trove boundary analysis

**Date**: 2026-07-16. **Data collection and diagnosis only — no solver code changed.** Direct
follow-up to `reports/2026-07-16-r02248-orientation-scoring-interaction.md`, whose own caveats
section flagged R02248 as "n=1 — whether this specific interaction generalizes to other
high-`reqInt`, high-`navDensity` levels is untested." This scans both stress corpora for levels
sharing R02248's profile, generates their 8 symmetry orientations, and tests each for the same
"some orientations resist the solver entirely" symptom.

## Method

Scanned `data/stress/stress-levels.json` + `data/stress/stress-levels-random.json` via
`scripts/stress/features.mjs`'s `levelFeatures` for `navDensity >= 0.82`
(`POLICY.NEAR_HAMILTONIAN_DENSITY`) and `reqInt >= 5`, excluding R02248 itself and every parent
already covered by the 38-family Experiment 1 rerun. 353 candidates matched broadly; narrowing to
levels with R02248's exact mechanic inventory (no must-cross/must-pass/portals/filters — just
blocks/geese/surround landmarks) left exactly **one** clean match: **R01465** (stress-corpus-2,
14×14, navDensity 0.83, `reqInt: 8`, 23 blocks, 5 geese, 3 surround landmarks — closely matching
R02248's 12×12/0.886/`reqInt: 7`/21 blocks/8 geese/3 surround profile). Widened the pool with two
more near-Hamiltonian/high-`reqInt` candidates carrying one additional mechanic each, for breadth:
**R02472** (mustCross=8, flippingFilters=6, otherwise clean, `reqInt: 16`) and **R02808**
(mustPass=5, turn-landmarks, `reqInt: 15`).

For each of the 3 candidates: generated its 7 symmetry siblings via `family-generate.mjs
--mode=symmetry` (same tool/mode as the original Experiment 1), then solved parent + all 7
siblings (`scripts/portfolio-solve-sweep.mjs --scheduler-mode=legacy --budget-ms=90000
--node-budget=8000000`, single-seed, no retries) — a smaller budget than R02248's original
investigation (up to 425s/271M nodes for its hardest variants) chosen to keep the scan tractable
across 3 candidates × 8 orientations; treated as sufficient to reproduce the *qualitative*
diagnostic signature (beam collapse at a handful of nodes vs. genuine timeout), not necessarily to
push every hard orientation all the way to a final verdict.

**Tooling note**: this scan is also what surfaced the `stress:benchmark`/`--levels` selector bugs
documented and fixed in the two commits right before this one — the family files this scan
generated are bare arrays, which is exactly the shape that crashed `stress:benchmark`.
`portfolio-solve-sweep.mjs` (position-based, no id-lookup ambiguity) was used instead throughout.

## Results

| Level | Parent | Siblings solved | Split? |
|---|---|---|---|
| R01465 | fails (node-budget-reached, 8M nodes) | 2/7 (variants 1, 7) | **Yes — 2/8 total** |
| R02472 | fails | 0/7 | No — 0/8, uniformly hard |
| R02808 | fails | 0/7 | No — 0/8, uniformly hard |

R02472 and R02808 (the two candidates carrying an extra mechanic beyond R02248's minimal
inventory) show no orientation-dependent split at all within this budget — every orientation times
out identically (`node-budget-reached` at ~8,000,000 nodes each). This is **inconclusive, not a
negative result**: it's equally consistent with "this phenomenon doesn't apply here" and "these two
are just harder overall (extra mechanics, higher `reqInt`) and need more than 90s/8M nodes to reach
the same beam-collapse signature R02248 and R01465 show at this budget." Not chased further — the
mechanic-clean R01465 is the informative data point.

## R01465: same symptom, different mechanism

Using R02248's own dihedral-group mapping (a property of `family-generate.mjs`'s shared
`transformPoint` transform code — variant 1/2/3 = rotation 90°/180°/270°, variant 4 = pure
reflection, variants 5/6/7 = reflection composed with 180°/270°/90° respectively — so it's fixed
by the generator, not re-derived per level): **variants 1 (r) and 7 (rs) solved; parent (e) and
variants 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (r², r³, s, r²s, r³s) all failed.** Unlike R02248 (where the split was a
clean rotation-only 4-vs-4: {0°,90°} easy, {180°,270°} hard, independent of reflection), R01465's
solved pair shares only the 90° rotation component — its own identity (0°, no reflection) orientation
fails, unlike R02248's.

Direct `Solver.solve` trace on R01465's parent (`timeBudgetMs: 60000, nodeBudget: 6000000`)
reproduces R02248's exact diagnostic signature:

| Attempt | Result | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| repair, seed 0 | fails | 2,000,005 nodes, timed out, `bestBadness: 20` |
| repair, seed 1 | fails | 2,000,006 nodes, timed out, `bestBadness: 9` |
| beam `intersectionHarvest@5000` | fails | **6 nodes — genuine exhaustion, not timeout** |
| beam `objectiveFirst@5000` | fails | **1 node — genuine exhaustion, not timeout** |
| DFS/LDS `intersectionHarvest` | fails | 52,913 nodes, timed out, `finalBadness: 28` |
| DFS/LDS `objectiveFirst` | fails | 154,743 nodes, timed out, `finalBadness: 19` |
| repair, full-budget fallback | fails | 1,792,354 nodes, timed out, `bestBadness: 20` |

Beam frontiers collapsing to single-digit node counts (not timeouts) and repair's `bestBadness`
plateauing across independent seeds is **exactly** R02248's signature. This confirms the general
phenomenon — an orientation-shaped early trajectory locking in a self-defeating choice on a
near-Hamiltonian level with surround landmarks — is not unique to R02248.

**But the responsible scoring term is different.** Disabling `SCORE_INTERSECTION_SETUP` (R02248's
universal unlock) does **not** unlock R01465 — beam attempts still collapse (1 node each, if
anything faster than baseline) and the level stays unsolved at the full 90s/8M-node budget. Testing
the other 3 flags R02248's own investigation had flagged (helping 2 of its 4 hard variants each,
without being the universal one there):

| Flag disabled | Result |
|---|---|
| `SCORE_INTERSECTION_SETUP` | fails (beam: 1, 1 nodes) |
| `SCORE_PHASE_SCALING` | fails (beam: 1, 22 nodes) |
| `SCORE_REVISIT_PENALTY` | fails (beam: 4, 1 nodes) |
| **`SCORE_SURROUND_URGENCY`** | **solves** — 133-step valid solution, beam `intersectionHarvest` in 4 nodes |
| all 4 disabled together | solves (same solution) |

`SCORE_SURROUND_URGENCY` — not `SCORE_INTERSECTION_SETUP` — is R01465's unlock. Both levels carry
surround landmarks (R02248: 3, R01465: 3), and R02248's own investigation had already noted
`SCORE_SURROUND_URGENCY` as a *partial* contributor there (unlocked 2 of its 4 hard variants,
alongside `SCORE_PHASE_SCALING`/`SCORE_REVISIT_PENALTY`) without being the dominant term. Here it's
R01465's dominant term instead.

## Interpretation

This upgrades the finding from "R02248, n=1, possibly a one-off" to: **a real, recurring failure
mode on near-Hamiltonian + nontrivial-`reqInt` + surround-landmark levels, but drawn from a small
family of related scoring terms rather than one universally-broken one.** `SCORE_INTERSECTION_SETUP`,
`SCORE_SURROUND_URGENCY`, `SCORE_PHASE_SCALING`, and `SCORE_REVISIT_PENALTY` all reward/penalize
choices that are appropriate on average but can compound with orientation-dependent early
trajectories into an unrecoverable lock on a level with near-zero slack — *which* term dominates
depends on level-specific detail (exact landmark placement, grid size) that this scan didn't have
budget to characterize further. Nothing here points at a specific, correct, corpus-safe fix; if
anything it argues against one — a fix narrow enough to help R02248 (targeting
`SCORE_INTERSECTION_SETUP`) would have done nothing for R01465, and vice versa.

## What this doesn't establish

- **Still not corpus-wide.** Two data points (R02248, R01465) sharing the exact mechanic profile,
  out of 353 broadly-matching candidates and 2,150 total stress-corpus levels. R02472/R02808's
  inconclusive (uniformly-hard, not split) results mean the "how common is this" question is open,
  not answered — a real answer would need either a much larger scan at R02248-scale budgets (hours,
  not the ~20 minutes this scan took) or a cheaper proxy signal for "will show the split" that
  hasn't been developed.
- **No fix proposed or made**, same reasoning as the original report: every flag involved is very
  likely load-bearing for other levels that genuinely need its incentive to reach their `reqInt`/
  surround-visitation requirement at all. Any change needs the same corpus-wide solvability+speed
  verification as any other solver hot-path change.
- **R01465's own solved/unsolved split is now committed as a symmetry family** (`data/families/
  family-R01465-symmetry{,-manifest}.json`, alongside R02472/R02808's) for anyone who wants to dig
  further without re-generating.
