# Solve-rate opportunity catalog: portal-excluded mechanisms and additive-tier repricing 001

> **Status:** active
> **Last evidence:** 2026-09-09 — current 975/1,700 Corpus-2 production run (`reports/stress/solver-corpus2-latest.json`, commit `045bbe90`) joined to the 2026-09-03 isolated capability map, plus two soundness/activation probes run at current HEAD
> **Decision:** four production search mechanisms are switched off by portal carve-outs on 954/1,700 Corpus-2 levels, which hold 551/725 current misses and 464/604 misses without an isolated winner. Two of the four now have first-screen soundness and activation evidence for portal levels. Test restoring them under matched work before building new propagation machinery for the same cohort. Separately, two default-ON retry tiers spend 23.8G `workSpent` (11.8% of all corpus work) re-running the ladder under flag changes that are already inert on portal levels.
> **Remaining gate:** per item below; the first two are matched-work A/Bs on prespecified portal-bearing populations, each requiring the full soundness/differential gate in [`../docs/solver-correctness-hardening.md`](../docs/solver-correctness-hardening.md).
> **Evidence role:** discovery (whole-population observational join, plus mechanism-level probes)
> **Selection:** observational — the whole 1,700-level corpus at the current production boundary; the portal split was chosen after reading the carve-out sites in the solver source, not after ranking candidate features by outcome

## Scope

Review of the solver system and its history for opportunities to raise cold, level-blind solve rate on the stress corpora. Everything below is either (a) a fact readable from current source, (b) a whole-population join of committed artifacts, or (c) a probe run at current HEAD and named as such. No production behaviour was changed; the two source patches used for probes were reverted (see [Reproduction](#reproduction)).

Numbers come from one frozen run — Corpus 2, 1,700 levels, `budgetMs=86,400,000`, `nodeBudget=50,000,000`, `workBudget=67,000,000`, sequential production engine — and are regenerated by [`../scripts/analyze-portal-carveout-exposure.mjs`](../scripts/analyze-portal-carveout-exposure.mjs) into [`stress/portal-carveout-exposure-2026-09-09.json`](stress/portal-carveout-exposure-2026-09-09.json).

Their median isolated-technique multiplicity is **0** against **8** for portal-free levels: on the portal side this is a capability gap, not only an action-selection gap.

Two framing facts from the same run:

- **No miss exhausted its search space.** Across all 725 misses and all 13 lifecycle stages, `exhaustedSearchSpace` is true zero times, and zero misses were deadline-truncated. Every failure is a budget/policy outcome, so search efficiency on the portal population has real headroom rather than being a proven-infeasible wall.
- **82% of all solver work is spent on levels that fail.** 166.1G of 201.9G total `workSpent` goes to the 725 misses; the 975 solves cost 35.8G. Median miss spends 233M work against a 23M median solve and a 341M most-expensive solve.

This reproduces the descriptive cohort in [`2026-09-08-routing-predicate-composition-and-capability-risk-001.md`](2026-09-08-routing-predicate-composition-and-capability-risk-001.md) (triple-overlap 29.8% solve rate) from a different direction and supplies a mechanism-level candidate cause for it.

## The portal carve-out inventory

Four production mechanisms are gated off entirely when `level.portalMap.size > 0`:

| # | Mechanism | Site | Gate | Origin of the exclusion |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Beam coarse-state merge (and its near-tie runner-up retention) | `modules/solver/search.ts` `useCoarseStateMerge` | `portalMap.size === 0` | `5d135d94b` (2026-06-12), on a single published level being *slower*: "portal usage not captured in sc" |
| 2 | Connectivity volume check | `modules/solver/topology.ts`, `isConnected` tail | `portalMap.size === 0` | predates the 2026-06-26 TypeScript migration; rationale is that portal jumps inflate `freshVolume` |
| 3 | Must-cross neighbour-budget propagation (`PRUNE_MC_NEIGHBOR_BUDGET`) | `modules/solver/lower-bounds.ts:123` | `portalMap.size > 0` | 2026-08-08 derivation: "portal forced-move semantics need separate validation" |
| 4 | Parity prune and parity gate filter | `modules/solver/hard-prune-pipeline.ts:79`, `modules/solver/orchestration.ts:590` | `portalMap.size === 0` | twist portals can repair parity |

There is direct precedent for treating these as untested rather than derived: `d87338d0f` (2026-07-31, "Include portal levels in the reserved-intersection wall") removed a fifth exclusion after showing it "conflated two different requirements" and was "conservatism inherited from the volume check's, not a derived requirement". That change shipped with 0 soundness rejections and unchanged published-corpus outcomes.

Mechanism 1's exclusion is the weakest by the repo's own current understanding. Coarse-state merge is explicitly **not** exact future-state equivalence — [`../docs/solver-architecture.md`](../docs/solver-architecture.md) calls it "width/retention management", it already discards the entire `visited` set, and [`../docs/solver-architectural-speed-opportunities.md`](../docs/solver-architectural-speed-opportunities.md) lists *removing* it as a closed negative because it "also performs survivor compression/diversity management" and costs real solves. The 2026-06-12 justification ("merged paths that had used different portals") is an argument for putting portal state **into** the tuple, which has since grown from 4 fields to 8 with a per-level mixed-radix encoding, not for disabling the mechanism on 56% of the corpus.

## Catalog

Ranked by expected solve-rate value per unit of evidence cost. Every item states the current limitation, the cheapest falsifying test, and the gate, per [`../docs/solver-future-work.md`](../docs/solver-future-work.md)'s backlog contract.

### A1. Portal-state coarse-state merge (highest expected value)

**Limitation.** On 954/1,700 levels every beam attempt runs with no frontier merging at all: width is spent on candidates that are coarse-state duplicates, and the near-tie runner-up retention that is default-ON everywhere else never engages.

**Change.** Extend the coarse-state tuple with the two portal fields the search state already carries — `portalJumps` (radix = portal pairs + 1) and `lastWasPortalJump` (radix 2) — and drop the `portalMap.size === 0` term. `BeamNode` already snapshots seven such scalars; `_composeBeamNumericCoarseStateKey`'s mixed-radix product and its `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` fallback to the exact delimited string key absorb two more factors unchanged. Because each portal pair can be traversed at most once (`search-state.ts:361` forbids re-entering a visited portal cell), `portalJumps` determines the remaining real path length, which is what `depth` alone fails to determine on a portal level. A stricter variant adds an explicit `portalUsedMask` to `SolverSearchState`; start with the two free fields.

**Cheapest falsifying test.** Fixed-work A/B on a prespecified portal-bearing Corpus-2 population, control = current HEAD, treatment = merge enabled with the extended tuple, identical work envelope. Report gains **and** losses; the 2026-08-28 exposure experiments show a beam change can starve a formerly protected winner.

**Gate.** Zero referee/regression failures, `solver:regression -- --check` unchanged on the published corpus, and a net solve gain with losses enumerated. Beam identity changes are correctness-hardening class 1: add counterexamples that vary only the new fields.

**Risk.** This changes search behaviour, not just cost; it is not a pure-speed change and must not be measured as one.

### A2. Connectivity volume check on portal levels — first screen already passed

**Limitation.** `isConnected`'s volume check (`freshVolume + intNeeded < rSteps → dead`) is skipped on every portal level, although `getRealLengthFromState` (`path.length - 1 - portalJumps`) is exact with portals and the flood fill already traverses portal edges.

**Derivation.** Completing the path takes `rSteps` more real steps plus some number of free portal jumps; every landing consumes a distinct fresh cell or a revisit, and revisits are bounded by `intNeeded` (portal jumps onto a visited cell charge an intersection exactly as ordinary moves do — the argument `d87338d0f` used for the reserved wall). So fresh landings ≥ `rSteps − intNeeded`, and all of them must lie in the reachable fresh set. The inequality's direction survives portals; the "inflating `freshVolume`" concern makes the prune weaker, not unsound.

**Probes run at HEAD (2026-09-09).**

- *Soundness:* removed the gate, ran `scripts/stress/connectivity-soundness-check.mjs` over all three corpora — **266,320 known-valid solution paths, 21,803,977 prefix states, 0 rejections**, identical verdict to the unpatched baseline (which was rerun first for comparability).
- *Activation:* on three portal-bearing must-cross production misses (`R00073`, `R00082`, `R00139` — the first three by corpus position matching portals ≥ 1, must-cross ≥ 2, currently unsolved), at `nodeBudget=2,000,000` and no repair additive budget, the newly-live check evaluated 0.77M–1.29M times per level and **rejected real search states 0 / 3,211 / 153 times**. It is live, unlike `PRUNE_PORTAL_PARITY_ENVELOPE`, which was closed as sound-but-non-firing.

**Remaining gate.** The rejection rate is real but modest (≤0.4% of evaluations here), so value is a matched-work A/B question, not a foregone conclusion. Also enable the mirrored gate in `isConnectedForFalseGoalTriggerSearch` only after a separate false-goal audit, since that path uses a deliberately stricter `maxVisit`.

### A3. Must-cross neighbour-budget propagation on portal levels — largest excluded population

**Limitation.** `mustCrossNeighborBudgetDeadlocked` returns `false` outright on portal levels. The currently-served population is 409 portal-free must-cross levels; the excluded population is **530** portal-bearing must-cross levels — larger than the one on which this prune was promoted with **+54 net (59 gained / 5 lost)** on Corpus 2 in 2026-08-12.

**Probes run at HEAD (2026-09-09).** With the portal term removed:

- *Soundness:* `scripts/stress/mc-neighbor-budget-soundness-check.mjs` — Corpus 2: 939 levels, 75,681 valid paths, 7,553,403 replayed steps, 5,803,556 evaluated states, **0 rejections on a real solution**; Corpus 1: 43 levels, 10,935 paths, 831,142 steps, **0 rejections**.
- *Activation:* on the same three portal levels, the prune evaluated 7.7M–9.1M times and **fired 399,463 / 321,377 / 557,214 times** (4.2%–6.1% of evaluations) — a substantial live prune currently switched off.

**Still to validate before any promotion.** The derivation's portal-specific obligations: (i) a portal terminal adjacent to a must-cross cell cannot serve as the straight-pass neighbour at all (entering it forces the jump), which is conservative in the safe direction but should be stated explicitly; (ii) a *visited* portal-cell neighbour is unenterable and therefore a hard wall, currently counted as a one-intersection revisit — again conservative; (iii) portal-jump arrivals charging an intersection is the load-bearing assumption and should get its own differential test rather than being inherited from `d87338d0f`'s prose.

**Gate.** Extend the oracle-labelled branch atlas to portal levels, then a matched-work Corpus-2 A/B on the 530-level population with gains/losses enumerated and the published corpus regression unchanged.

### A4. Parity prune on same-parity-only portal levels (small, cheap, bounded)

A portal pair whose terminals share cell parity cannot repair a parity mismatch, so the ordinary parity invariant survives from step 1 on levels where every pair is same-parity — a different and much earlier trigger than `PRUNE_PORTAL_PARITY_ENVELOPE`'s "all twist pairs consumed" condition, which was closed as non-firing. The same argument re-enables the parity **gate filter** in `orchestration.ts:590`, which currently returns all gates unfiltered on any portal level.

**Sizing (measured):** only **21 of 954** portal-bearing Corpus-2 levels have zero twist pairs (9 currently solved). Do this as a cheap correctness/coverage cleanup bundled with A2, not as a solve-rate bet on its own; a separate A/B is not worth buying for a 21-level population.

### B1. Stop paying full additive budget for flag-inert retry tiers

Two default-ON retry tiers toggle mechanisms that the portal carve-outs have already disabled, and their eligibility predicates contain no portal term:

| Tier | Toggles | Portal-bearing: work / dispatches / wins | Portal-free: work / dispatches / wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| `must-cross-neighbor-prune-disabled-retry` | `PRUNE_MC_NEIGHBOR_BUDGET` (inert when portals > 0) | **14.5G / 359 / 3** | 4.2G / 109 / 6 |
| `coarse-state-near-tie-retention-disabled-retry` | `STRATEGY_COARSE_STATE_NEAR_TIE_RETENTION` (merge off when portals > 0) | **9.3G / 643 / 7** | 3.2G / 225 / 30 |

**23.8G — 11.8% of all corpus work — is spent re-running the ladder under a flag change that cannot alter search behaviour on those levels.** `stage-budget.ts`'s own comment for the first tier reasons that skipping an ineligible level is "free in solves and free in cost"; the predicate simply does not know about the portal carve-out.

**Important honesty constraint.** These dispatches still won 10 levels, because each tier draws a *fresh additive work pool*, so on a portal level the tier is an anonymous extra-budget pass rather than a mechanism test. Removing the dispatch is therefore **not** free: the correct experiment is an equal-work reallocation A/B (same total envelope, the freed work given to a mechanism that is live on portal levels — naturally A1/A2/A3), not an unconditional deletion. Note also that per-tier attribution is currently distorted: any solve credited to these tiers on a portal level is evidence about budget, not about the named mechanism.

If A1 and A3 land, both tiers become genuinely meaningful on portal levels and this item resolves itself — which is an argument for sequencing B1 after A1/A3 rather than in parallel.

### B2. Reprice the additive envelope with the full-population marginal-value ladder

Every additive retry tier holds `BUDGET_FRACTION = 1.0` — each gets a fresh copy of the whole solve's work budget. The nominal `workBudget` is 67M; the median miss spends 233M and the p90 miss 279M. That ~3.5× envelope was never designed; it accumulated one individually-additive, "costs nothing to earlier solves" promotion at a time, which is exactly what [`../docs/solver-optimization-workstreams.md`](../docs/solver-optimization-workstreams.md)'s "new actions do not get free additive budget" rule exists to prevent.

**Interpretation limit, stated up front:** these ratios are confounded by ladder position — a late tier only ever sees levels every earlier tier failed, so its average cost per win is not directly comparable to `main-ladder`'s. The ladder is a *nomination* device, not a verdict. `guidance-goal-distance-retry` is nonetheless the clearest candidate: 17.4% of all corpus work, more than the entire main ladder, for 6 wins.

**Cheapest falsifying test.** For the top candidate, a frozen fixed-aggregate-envelope A/B that reduces its fraction and gives the freed work to the tiers immediately around it, with specialist-retention accounting (the 2026-09-04 tail audits show these tiers hold a small number of genuinely unique wins). This is exactly the WS2A repricing shape already in flight for three other candidates and should join that queue rather than starting a new lane.

### B3. Portal-aware mechanic-bucket retention

`_mechanicBucketSelect` buckets the frontier by `(flipperUsedMask, mustCrossMask)` and guarantees each bucket a share of the width. On portal levels the most obvious structural diversity axis — which portal transitions the path has taken — is not a bucket, and coarse merge is off, so the frontier can collapse onto one portal strategy with nothing preserving alternatives. Deferred behind A1: if the extended coarse-state tuple already preserves portal-distinct survivors, this may be unnecessary. Reopen only if A1 lands and portal-strategy collapse is still observable in beam traces.

### C1. Stage-lifecycle telemetry reports two live stages as never instantiated

`orchestration.ts`'s `finish()` builds `mechanicallyEligible`/`instantiated` from a hand-written `instantiated` map that has no entry for `guidance-goal-distance-retry` or `late-repair-multiseed-retry`, both added later (stage orders 125 and 128). Both therefore report `mechanicallyEligible: false, instantiated: false` in persisted telemetry **while actually running** — on this run, 725 and 187 of the 725 misses respectively, consuming 35.0G and 23.4G of work.

This is correctness-hardening class 11 (telemetry projection drift) and it directly corrupts the opportunity-population sizing that [`../docs/solver-experiment-opportunity-sizing.md`](../docs/solver-experiment-opportunity-sizing.md) and `scripts/experiment-opportunity-audit.mjs` depend on: the two most expensive additive tiers look non-existent to any eligibility-based population count.

**Fix (canonical, not another hand-written row).** Derive `instantiated` from `solverStageSpec(id).attemptSource`, which `stage-policy.ts` already declares for every stage (`configured-main` → `hasMainConfig`, `configured-repair` → `hasRepairConfig`, `admissible-order-profiles` → the admissible-order predicate), preserving `late-repair-search`'s documented inversion. Add a projection test that fails when a stage present in `SOLVER_STAGE_IDS` is missing from the mapping — the same failure mode would otherwise recur on the next stage added.

**Note for readers of existing artifacts:** analyses that filtered on `mechanicallyEligible` and included either stage are affected; the `reached`/`actualWork` fields on the same rows are correct.

### C2. The in-envelope stratum's capability number is a month stale

`reports/stress/benchmark-envelope-latest.json` reports 124/200 from **2026-08-06**, at commit `f73a515c`, with `budgetMs=60,000`, `nodeBudget=20,000,000` and **no work budget** — a different code base and a materially different envelope from the Corpus-2 run every current decision cites. The envelope stratum is the one that answers "can the solver handle complexity users can actually construct", and it is 53.5% portal-bearing (107/200), so it is directly exposed to every item above.

**Action.** Refresh it at the current production envelope before A1/A3 land, so there is a pre-change baseline on the player-relevant stratum, then again after. 200 levels is a cheap run by this repo's standards.

### C3. Feed the unresolved Jul-29 → Aug-6 portal-difficulty forensic question

[`2026-09-05-routing-regime-archetype-risk-three-point-refinement-001.md`](2026-09-05-routing-regime-archetype-risk-three-point-refinement-001.md) established that multi-portal enrichment moved 1.015 → 1.168 → 1.250 across three snapshots and explicitly left the cause unidentified, naming commit-log correlation as the missing step. This catalog supplies a mechanism-level hypothesis worth testing against that history: **capability accretion that structurally excludes portal levels widens the gap without any portal-specific regression.** `PRUNE_MC_NEIGHBOR_BUDGET` is the clean instance — promoted 2026-08-12 with +54 net on Corpus 2, portal levels excluded by construction — and fits the Aug-6 → Sep-3 widening rather than the earlier jump. The Jul-29 → Aug-6 step still needs its own bisection; the window contains the canonical work-unit budget migration, the must-cross forced-structure prunes, and the reserved-wall portal inclusion.

## What this catalog deliberately does not propose

Checked and left closed: removing coarse beam dedup; exact DFS/beam transposition caching; broad repair-fallback gate widening to `multi-portal` (0 gains / 2 losses, 2026-08-23); `PRUNE_PORTAL_PARITY_ENVELOPE` in its tested all-twist-consumed form; the append-last and reserve-preserving high-intersection beam exposures; universal beam-width increases; new scoring profiles whose novelty is weights; and the richer static topology/placement selector bundle (no held-out value, 2026-09-08). Items A1–A4 are not "reopen a closed negative": none of these four mechanisms was ever tested on portal levels — three of the four exclusions predate the evidence standard the repo now applies, and the fourth names portal validation as future work in its own derivation.

Relationship to the current WS1 gate: the observer-only joint-obligation propagation in [`2026-09-09-joint-obligation-propagation-and-residual-lane-handoff-001.md`](2026-09-09-joint-obligation-propagation-and-residual-lane-handoff-001.md) targets the same cohort with new machinery. A1–A3 are the cheaper prior hypothesis for why that cohort collapses — three already-validated mechanisms are simply not running there — and should be classified before an unexplained-reasoning-failure population is used to justify a new representation.
