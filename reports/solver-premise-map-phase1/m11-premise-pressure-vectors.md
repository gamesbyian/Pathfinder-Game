Dimensions are: graph degree, unresolved descendants, contradiction/tension membership, independent-discovery lineage, evidence thinness, architecture centrality, freshness risk, failed tested forms beneath an open parent, and affected loci/interfaces. Values below are deliberately heterogeneous because the preregistration forbids pretending these dimensions are commensurable.

## Unusual vector shapes

### Shape 1 — structural hub with mixed evidence: P032

P032 combines the highest reported degree with two failed compact forms and a still-open parent. Its pressure is concentrated in semantic breadth and decomposition uncertainty, not simple evidence absence. The vector warns against either closing the parent from child failures or treating its degree as an argument to keep generating arbitrary forms.

Alternative interpretation: the degree itself may be inflated by deliberate hardening around an already controversial family. Confidence: high on shape, medium on causal significance.

### Shape 2 — diagnostic hub whose main weakness is observability: P024

P024 has substantial cross-locus reach but comparatively little direct mechanism content. Its unusual vector is high graph/architecture centrality plus evidence thinness caused by inability to localize the first loss reliably. This differs from P032's representation problem.

Alternative interpretation: witness-relative lineage instrumentation may be too expensive to justify universal deployment; an offline sample could be enough. Confidence: high.

### Shape 3 — controller parent with architecture/freshness pressure: P063

P063 is structurally central while the object it names, a general adaptive replanning loop, is not cleanly deployed. The vector is dominated by architecture centrality and freshness risk because every new scheduler/action family changes the feasible controller state/action space.

Alternative interpretation: the proposition may be an umbrella over several smaller controllers rather than one missing component. Confidence: medium-high.

### Shape 4 — mature phenomenon with a value-interface tension: P040

P040 is an important comparator because its evidence is comparatively strong. Its pressure comes not from whether ranking matters, but from C004 and the prefer→retain interface: a mature local phenomenon can still sit next to an unresolved consumption contract.

Alternative interpretation: a set-aware ranking objective may absorb the interface issue without changing the parent. Confidence: high.

### Shape 5 — low-local-evidence, high-governance reach: P198

P198 has independent-reconstruction lineage and broad decision reach, but it is not a runtime capability claim. Its vector demonstrates why pressure dimensions cannot be summed: high architecture/research reach plus novelty does not imply a solver feature should be built.

Alternative interpretation: maturity gates are documentation discipline already implicit in good experiments rather than a research object. Confidence: high.

### Shape 6 — orthogonal parent from independent reconstruction: P199/P200

P199 and P200 have independent lineage, high freshness sensitivity, and cross-interface reach, yet their graph structure is not the main reason they matter. They reorganize existing local questions by generalization unit and information lifetime. Their pressure is ontological and compositional rather than degree-driven.

Alternative interpretation: both may remain synthesis parents whose value is explanatory, with no single direct experiment or implementation. Confidence: high.

## M11 bounded conclusion

The vector view falsifies the idea that “pressure” is one scalar quantity. P032, P024, P063, P040, P198, P199, and P200 can all look important for mutually different reasons. A weighted aggregate would silently encode policy choices about whether structural centrality, uncertainty, freshness, independent rediscovery, or failed forms deserve more weight.

M11 therefore records shapes only. It produces no ranking, tier, queue order, or aggregate pressure score, and none of these vectors is fed into M12 as a search priority.
