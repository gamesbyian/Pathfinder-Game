# Behavioral-state quotient probe result 001

> **Status:** concluded-negative
> **Last evidence:** 2026-09-17 — offline re-analysis of two already-committed per-state artifacts, current HEAD, zero new solver compute and zero new CP-SAT queries.
> **Decision:** Moonshot G's premise ("two syntactically different states are equivalent for search if every relevant future completion behavior available from one is available from the other... a probe-defined approximation can reveal which state distinctions matter") gets two different answers from its two available populations. On **B2** (28 states, 14 parent levels, true exact CP-SAT LIVE/DEAD labels), the natural candidate signature — the 4-tuple of pending must-cross/must-pass/flipper/portal-pair obligation counts already computed by H1 — **mixes LIVE and DEAD within the same board** in 3 of 9 multi-member groups: this is a direct, board-controlled falsification, not a generalization concern, so no held-out-parent split is needed to trust it. On **Card-E** (156 states, 156 distinct levels, proxy rescuability label from a bounded-node-budget search, not exact LIVE/DEAD), the same *style* of discrete signature (excluding remaining length, H3's known dominant driver) shows only 1 mixed group out of 26 multi-member (cross-parent, by construction) groups — far purer than a random-shuffle null (mean 6.6 mixed, p=0.0008) or a length-quintile-blocked null that preserves the length-outcome relationship (mean 4.7 mixed, p=0.0056). The signature carries real information beyond chance and beyond remaining length, but it is a *correlate of a proxy label*, not a demonstration of exact behavioral equivalence.
> **Remaining gate:** none earned. Per Moonshot G's own advancement bar (implicit: "how much syntactic state collapses without mixing known LIVE/DEAD behavior" — a strict bar), the exact-label test (B2) fails outright; the proxy-label test (Card-E) is suggestive but neither exact nor complete (74% of multi-member groups are pure only in the trivial sense of matching the population's 89%-non-reconstructable base rate, and the permutation test — not raw purity counts — is what actually separates signal from base-rate artifact here). Nothing here earns canonicalization, retention, or heuristic use.
> **Evidence role:** discovery, over two already-committed populations. Neither population's underlying labels or features were touched; this pass only computes a new discrete signature from already-recorded fields and groups/permutes against an already-fixed outcome.
> **Population identity:** Population A = the B2 28-state/14-parent-level exact-CP-SAT population (`reports/stress/h1-event-feasibility-queries-2026-09-16.json`, H1's own frozen replay), reused unmodified. Population B = Card-E's 156-row population (`reports/stress/h3-repair-commitment-interface-2026-09-17.json`, H3's own commitment-feature extraction), reused unmodified.
> **Selection history:** both populations' selection/labelling predate and are unrelated to this question (B2: 2026-08-12 sibling harvest + September CP-SAT resolution; Card-E: 2026-09-16 sizing pass). This report adds no new selection pressure — it only recomputes a discrete grouping key from already-recorded fields.
> **Inference scope:** this establishes that one specific, simple discrete obligation-count signature is not an exact behavioral quotient (falsified on B2) but does correlate with Card-E's proxy rescuability label beyond chance and beyond a known confound (falsification does not generalize to "no signature could ever work"; the positive does not generalize to "this is production-ready" or "this is exact"). It does not test other signature forms (canonical state-family, branch-decision signature) Moonshot G also names, nor a genuinely independent confirmation split of Card-E (its 156 levels are used in full for both defining and testing the signature, since the signature itself was fixed a priori from H3's own commitment fields, not tuned against the outcome).

## Why this ran

This is the last item in the frozen live-queue order in `docs/solver-optimization-workstreams.md` (behind topology, H1, DEAD-core, H3, H2). Its premise and minimal test come from `reports/2026-09-16-assumption-breaking-solver-development-moonshots-001.md`'s Moonshot G ("learn the state space, not merely the score"): "Sample sibling states, run a fixed battery of exact bounded completion/event probes, cluster by behavioral response, and ask how much syntactic state collapses without mixing known LIVE/DEAD behavior on held-out parents." A scoping pass found this premise had never been given a tracked question ID and, more importantly, that its exact ingredients — per-state pending-obligation counts, joined to already-resolved exact LIVE/DEAD labels (B2) or an already-resolved rescuability proxy (Card-E) — already exist as committed artifacts from H1 and H3, needing no new CP-SAT compute for a first pass (per the moonshot's own "cheapest falsifier" framing).

## What was implemented

**`scripts/stress/behavioral-state-quotient-probe.mjs`**: loads H1's committed B2 state-replay JSON (28 states, `pendingCounts` per state, `exactLabel` live/dead, `levelId` parent) and H3's committed Card-E commitment-feature JSON (156 rows, one per distinct level, `reconstructable` label), and for each population groups rows by a natural, a-priori discrete signature (no feature engineered post-hoc against either outcome). For B2 it reports mixed vs. pure vs. cross-parent-pure groups directly (population too small, at 14 parents, for a meaningful held-out split — same population-size caveat as the DEAD-core pilot). For Card-E it runs two permutation tests (5,000 trials each, seeded for exact reproducibility): a raw label shuffle, and a length-quintile-blocked shuffle that preserves the already-known length-outcome relationship, to test whether the signature's apparent purity survives controlling for H3's own dominant confound.

## Result

**B2 (exact labels):** 28 states collapse to 18 distinct signatures; 9 groups have more than one member. Of those 9, **3 mix LIVE and DEAD on the same board** (`S00001`, `S00048`, `R00104` — each contributing one `top-rank1` vs. `witness-culled` pair with identical pending-obligation counts but opposite exact labels). Exactly 1 group is a genuine cross-parent pure match: `S00095` and `S00099` both show signature `(0,0,0,1)` and are both LIVE.

**Card-E (proxy label, excludes remaining length):** 156 rows collapse to 113 distinct signatures; 26 groups have more than one member (all cross-parent, since each level contributes exactly one row). Only 1 of 26 is mixed. A random-shuffle null (same group structure, same 17/156 marginal rate) puts the expected mixed-group count at 6.6 (p=0.0008 for ≤1 mixed by chance); a length-quintile-blocked null (preserves the length→outcome relationship within each quintile) puts it at 4.7 (p=0.0056) — the observed purity survives controlling for length.

## Interpretation

The two populations are not in tension; they test different, complementary claims. B2's test is the strict one Moonshot G's wording literally asks for — *exact* equivalence, checked on the same board, where any confound from a different board is impossible. It fails cleanly: pending-obligation counts alone omit decision-relevant information (very likely exact cell identity or path-shape/position details, not just how many obligations remain) even holding the board fixed. Card-E's test is a looser, more generic claim — does a simple discrete feature tuple, when it happens to recur *by chance across unrelated boards*, correlate with a real (if noisy, budget-derived) outcome more than random chance would predict, independent of the already-known dominant length effect. It succeeds, modestly: there is real, board-independent, non-length information in `(mustCrossPending, mustPassPending, portalJumpsUsed, ints)` jointly, beyond what any single one of those features showed in H3's own marginal Cohen's-d ranking (all ≤0.39).

## What survives

The Card-E cross-parent partial-purity result is preserved as capability-memory evidence: a simple, prespecified discrete commitment tuple correlates with rescuability across independent boards beyond chance and beyond the length confound. This is a genuine nomination for a future, better-powered pass (a length-matched design, or a held-out confirmation split once a larger proxy-labelled population exists) — not, on its own, grounds for canonicalization, retention, or heuristic use per Moonshot G's own bar. The B2 same-parent cross-parent-pure pair (`S00095`/`S00099`) is too small (n=2) to nominate anything beyond noting it exists.

## Relation to existing evidence

This does not reopen H1 (event feasibility, closed negative on a different, richer event vocabulary) or H3 (commitment interface, closed negative for predicting rescuability *better than remaining length*) — both already established that none of these commitment-style features dominate length. This report asks a narrower, different question (does a *coarse, recurring signature* avoid mixing outcomes at all, independent of predicting them best) and finds a real but modest positive answer only for the cross-parent, proxy-labelled, length-controlled form of that question — consistent with, not contradicting, H3's own ranking of these same features as secondary effects.
