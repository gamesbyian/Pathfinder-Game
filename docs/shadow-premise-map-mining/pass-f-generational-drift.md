# Pass F — generational encoding drift

## F1 — proposition schema changes what kinds of questions can be represented directly

The 92-row baseline register has fields for:

- dependencies;
- interactions;
- adjacent unasked questions;
- architecture epoch.

The three extension files instead use:

- claim type;
- scope;
- system locus;
- mechanic/resources.

Those are not cosmetic substitutions. The baseline is better equipped to record neighborhood-style research context, while extensions are better equipped to position a proposition along cross-cutting semantic dimensions.

Representation consequence: an apparent absence of `claim_type` or `system_locus` in baseline rows is not missing conceptual content in the same sense as an empty extension field. The schema simply did not encode it.

Classification: representation limitation; very high confidence.

## F2 — later relation generations broaden from conceptual edges into epistemic/evidential edges

The untyped seed graph records pair adjacency. v2 introduces a defined typed relation vocabulary. v3 adds relation types for confounding, evidence against, non-falsification, alternative explanation, assumptions, measurement dependence, and strengthening.

This means the map becomes increasingly capable of representing **how a claim should change interpretation of another claim**, not just how concepts are related.

A later proposition's richer connectivity can therefore be partly an ontology effect.

Classification: representation-sensitive; high confidence.

## F3 — proposition IDs stop being the sole unit of graph expression

v3 uses prose endpoints and F-numbered failure labels alongside proposition IDs. This expands expressivity but weakens closed-world graph assumptions.

The shift also changes what “missing proposition” means: a concept can be present relationally without receiving a P-number.

Classification: representation limitation; high confidence.

## F4 — four extension rows are not valid 17-column CSV under standard quoting rules

A standard CSV parse of the frozen canonical files finds field-count mismatches in:

- `solver-premise-space-extension-2026-09-17b.csv`: **P189** (18 fields), **P192** (19 fields);
- `solver-premise-space-extension-2026-09-17c.csv`: **P198** (18 fields), **P199** (19 fields).

The cause is unquoted commas in later free-text fields such as solve-count links. Human reading remains possible, but standards-compliant field-indexed tooling shifts subsequent columns.

This matters directly to mining. For example, a naive parser can misassign `gap_type`, `claim_type`, `scope`, `system_locus`, `mechanic_resources`, and `source_paths` on those rows.

This is not a conceptual finding about solver research. It is a substrate defect that can manufacture analytical artifacts.

Classification: representation defect; very high confidence.

## F5 — later generations are more abstract on average in their final rows, but chronology cannot establish causation

The last extension layer contains high-level parents such as information authority (P197), research maturity (P198), generalization unit (P199), and information half-life/transfer radius (P200). These are cross-cutting abstractions rather than narrow mechanism treatments.

It is tempting to conclude that the map “became more abstract over time,” but the frozen object alone cannot distinguish:

- genuine conceptual learning;
- deliberate allocation of later files to completeness/hardening work;
- representational affordances introduced by the newer schema;
- simple topic-ordering effects.

The defensible finding is only that **generation and abstraction level are entangled in this snapshot**.

Classification: representation-sensitive; medium confidence.

## Negative results

- Schema evolution does not imply that baseline propositions are inferior or semantically obsolete.
- No clean quantitative trend over all 142 rows was established that would justify treating file generation as a maturity score.
- The pass does not infer developer intent from file order or consult commit history after the evidence boundary.

## Pass-F takeaway

The map is not one homogeneous dataset frozen at one representational maturity. It is a **stack of schema generations**. Some patterns in scope, relation richness, abstraction, or missing metadata can be generated by that stack rather than by the solver research space itself. The CSV field-count defects make this more than a philosophical caution: mechanically mined results can actually be corrupted.
